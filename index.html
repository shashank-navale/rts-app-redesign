01 The problem

A 1.1★ app riders double-check with Google Maps

Riders open it, don't trust it, and check a second app.

  • 1.1★ average across 235 App Store reviews.
  • Vanishing buses. Trackers drop off the map when buses detour.
  • Prior knowledge required. You must already know a stop number or route name.

02 Research

Five riders, five needs

I interviewed five riders and clustered the notes into five needs.

What riders need

  1. N1 Arrival estimates I can trust
  2. N2 A heads-up when a bus is late, missing, or done for the night
  3. N3 Feeling safe while waiting and getting home at night
  4. N4 Finding my route without knowing the system
  5. N5 An app that loads and responds on the go

03 The decision

24 ideas → 2 concepts → 1 bet

I scored each idea 1–10 on how New, Useful and Feasible it is (NUF).

Why HeadsUp

It targets the two failures riders hit daily: wrong arrival times and vanishing buses. Five riders reviewed both concepts, and their preferences matched the scores.

HeadsUp

Chosen

For Maya, the daily commuter

Warns her about her bus before she's at the stop.

  • Leave-by alert
  • Vanished-bus alert
  • Status tag on every bus
  • One-tap backup plan

NUF 91

Where2

For Jordan, the occasional rider

Say where you're going, get one clear route.

  • Destination search
  • Nearest stops on your route
  • Plain-language steps
  • Last-bus-tonight countdown

NUF 89

Why not Where2, yet

Its destination search and plain-language steps suit new riders, and can build on HeadsUp later.

05 Where it stands

Validated direction, next: usability testing

Validated

Five potential riders reviewed both concepts, and their preferences matched the scoring.

Next

  1. Run a usability test on the wireframes

What I learned

The hardest call was choosing between concepts two points apart; checking with riders made it defensible.
